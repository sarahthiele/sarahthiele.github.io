About Me

I'm a PhD candidate in Princeton University's Department of Astrophysical Sciences, working with Gáspár Bakos on detecting, characterizing, and differentiating between satellites and near-Earth objects in wide-field images from the HATPI telescope at Las Campanas Observatory, Chile.

I am also a Junior Fellow of the Outer Space Institute, where I engage in efforts to promote the security and sustainability of the space environment. I am particularly interested in the policy dimensions of large satellite constellations, including their collision and orbital debris risks, and their impact on astronomical observations and dark skies.

Research Interests

My interests sits at the intersection of observational astronomy and space sustainability. On the technical side, I build machine learning pipelines for detecting and characterizing satellite trails in wide-field imaging data. On the policy side, I contribute to advocacy and regulatory frameworks aimed at space debris mitigation and protecting dark and quiet skies. Additionally, I'm passionate about science communication and outreach, and seek to make astrophysics and space policy accessible and exciting to broad audiences.
