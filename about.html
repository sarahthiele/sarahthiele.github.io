About Me

I'm a PhD candidate in Princeton University's Department of Astrophysical Sciences, working with Gáspár Bakos on satellite trail detection and characterization in wide-field images from the HATPI telescope at Las Campanas Observatory, Chile.

I am also a Junior Fellow of the Outer Space Institute, where I engage in efforts to promote the security and sustainability of the space environment. I am particularly interested in the policy dimensions of large satellite constellations and their impact on astronomical observations and dark skies.

I'm passionate about science communication and outreach — finding ways to make astrophysics and space policy accessible, urgent, and exciting to broad audiences.

Research Interests

My work sits at the intersection of observational astronomy and space sustainability. On the technical side, I build machine learning pipelines for detecting and characterizing satellite trails in wide-field imaging data. On the policy side, I contribute to advocacy and regulatory proceedings aimed at protecting dark and quiet skies.

Beyond Research

Outside the telescope, I'm interested in science policy, space law, and the governance of the orbital environment. I also enjoy painting, cooking, and travel.
